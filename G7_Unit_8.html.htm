Sorting the Connectors

Student B used conjunctions to combine ideas and remove repeated words. In Unit 8, we divide these conjunctions into two jobs: Addition (adding facts) and Purpose (explaining why).

Investigating the Rules: Addition

Let's look closely at how professional writers use these addition conjunctions. There are hidden grammar patterns you need to find.

🕵️ Find the Pattern

Example 1: She not only studied math but also practiced piano.

Example 2: The new train both cuts travel time and reduces traffic.

Question A: Look at the underlined verbs. What do you notice about the verbs on BOTH sides of the conjunctions?

Example 3: As well as practicing daily, the team ate healthy food.

Question B: What happens to the verb immediately after "as well as"?

Rule A (The Balance Rule): For paired conjunctions (not only... but also, both... and), the verbs on both sides must match in tense and form (parallelism).

Rule B (The -ing Rule): The verb immediately following as well as must be in the -ing form (gerund).

Investigating the Rules: Purpose

Now let's look at conjunctions of purpose. These explain why an action was done.

🕵️ Find the Pattern

Example 1: Rangers tagged the turtles so that scientists could track them.

Example 2: The school extended breaks in order that students might join clubs.

Example 3: Athletes train at high altitudes in order to improve their stamina.

Example 4: We left early so as to avoid the traffic.

Question: Discuss with your partner. Why do Examples 1 & 2 use words like "could" and "might", while Examples 3 & 4 do not?

The Modal Rule: Use a modal verb (can, could, will, would, might) + base verb after so that and in order that.

The Base Verb Rule: Use a base verb directly after in order to and so as to.

Be the Teacher: Peer Editing

Other students have tried to write summary sentences, but they made mistakes with the rules we just discovered.

✍️ Spot and Fix the Errors

Work with your group to find the grammar mistake in each sentence. Rewrite the corrected sentence in the box below.

  1. The team not only practiced daily but also were eating healthy food.
  2. As well as finish his homework, he cleaned his room.
  3. They saved their allowance in order to they could buy new bicycles.

1. The team not only practiced daily but also ate healthy food. (Balance Rule)

2. As well as finishing his homework, he cleaned his room. (-ing Rule)

3. They saved their allowance in order to buy new bicycles. (OR: ...so that they could buy...)
