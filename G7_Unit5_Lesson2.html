Collocations & Using Colons

Cambridge Grammar and Writing Skills — Learner's Book 8

Grammar Focus: Dependent Prepositions (Collocations)
Certain nouns and adjectives are paired with specific prepositions. These fixed combinations are known as collocations. Always memorize the word together with its dependent preposition.
1. Noun + Preposition Collocations
Preposition Common Nouns Example Sentence
FOR reason, demand, need, respect, cure There is a growing demand for renewable energy.
IN increase, decrease, rise, fall, interest We observed a sharp increase in temperature.
TO solution, answer, damage, reaction Scientists found a solution to the equation.
OF cause, picture, map, photo, advantage What was the primary cause of the accident?
WITH connection, relationship, contact She maintains a good connection with her peers.
2. Adjective + Preposition Collocations
Preposition Common Adjectives Example Sentence
AT good, bad, clever, brilliant He is exceptionally good at solving puzzles.
IN interested, experienced, involved Are you interested in creative writing?
OF afraid, proud, fond, aware, full They are immensely proud of their progress.
FOR famous, responsible, ready, suitable The region is famous for its historic tea gardens.
ABOUT excited, worried, anxious, passionate The students are excited about the upcoming trip.
TO married, similar, accustomed, addicted This outcome is very similar to our first test.
The Preposition + Gerund (-ing) Rule:
When a verb follows a preposition, it must take the -ing form (gerund).
Example: She is responsible for organizing the event (NOT for organize).
3. Punctuation Focus: Colons :
What is a Colon?
A colon (:) signals a pause and directs the reader's attention forward to what follows. It acts like a signpost saying "look at this next part."
Colon Function Rule & Explanation Example Sentence
1. Introducing a List Use a colon to introduce a list, but only after a complete independent clause. The recipe requires three main ingredients: flour, sugar, and butter.
2. Introducing an Explanation Use a colon to connect two clauses when the second clause explains or illustrates the first. The team had only one goal: they wanted to win the championship.
3. Direct Quotations & Emphasis Use a colon before a formal statement, direct quote, or single emphatic word. Remember the main rule of writing: clarity.

⚠️ Crucial Colon Rules & Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rule 1: Must Follow an Independent Clause
    A colon must always be preceded by a complete sentence that can stand alone.
  • My favorite fruits are: apples, bananas, and grapes. (Incorrect: "are" is a verb leading into the list)
  • My favorite fruits are apples, bananas, and grapes. (Correct: No colon needed)
  • I love three types of fruit: apples, bananas, and grapes. (Correct: Preceded by a complete clause)
  • We traveled through: France, Italy, and Spain. ➔ We traveled through three countries: France, Italy, and Spain.
