Semester 1 Grammar Reference Guide

Task I: Present Simple vs. Present Perfect

• Present Simple: Regular habits, routines, or general truths (e.g., "The choir sings every morning.").

• Present Perfect: Actions completed over a timeframe up to now or recent achievements (e.g., "The band has released three albums this year.").

• Present Perfect Passive: Subject receives an action over time (e.g., "The hall has been filled with guests.").

Task II: Past Tense Chronology (Timelines)

• Past Perfect (had + V3): Earliest Event (1) — Happened BEFORE another past action.

• Past Continuous (was/were + -ing): Action in progress in the past when interrupted.

• Simple Past (V2): Next / Latest Event (2 or 3) — Completed single past action.

Example: "By the time the alarm rang [2], she had already left [1]."

Task III: Pronouns in Narrative Context

• Relative Pronouns: whose (possession), where (place), that/which (things), who (people).

• Indefinite Pronouns: everyone, anyone, nobody, someone.

• Demonstratives: this, that, these, those, it.

Task IV: Connectives & Transitions

• Time & Sequence: After / Following (+ noun or gerund), First / Firstly, subsequently, By the time / By [year], Whenever.

• Simultaneous & Outcome: Meanwhile (at the same time), Eventually (in the end / after time).

Task V: Apostrophe Rules (Possessives vs. Contractions)

• Singular Possessive: Add 's (e.g., Mrs. Smith's notes).

• Plural Possessive ending in 's': Add apostrophe after 's' (e.g., the students' books).

• Its vs. It's: It's = contraction of 'it is'. Its = possessive pronoun (no apostrophe!).
