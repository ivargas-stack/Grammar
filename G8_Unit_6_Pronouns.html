Concept Discussion: Pronouns

Review the grammar rules below before starting the 15-item practice activity.

1. Demonstrative Pronouns
Demonstrative pronouns point out specific people, places, or things. They depend on physical or temporal distance (near vs. far) and quantity (singular vs. plural).
Distance Singular Plural
Near (close in space or time) this these
Far (distant in space or time) that those
Examples:
• This is the book I am reading right now.
• Look at those birds flying across the distant field.
2. Indefinite Pronouns
Indefinite pronouns refer to people, places, or things without specifying exactly who or what they are.
Category Pronouns
People someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, no one, nobody, everyone, everybody
Things something, anything, nothing, everything
Places somewhere, anywhere, nowhere, everywhere
Examples:
• Someone left a notebook on the kitchen table.
• She searched everywhere for her lost pencil.
3. Relative Pronouns
Relative pronouns connect a clause or phrase to a noun or pronoun. They provide essential or extra information about the subject or object.
Pronoun Used for... Example
who / whom People The teacher who taught us writing...
which / that Things / Animals The journal which was hidden...
whose Possession The girl whose book was found...
where Places The tree where she sits to write...
when Time The hour when the house grows quiet...
