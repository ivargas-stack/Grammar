1. Past Continuous Tense

We use the Past Continuous to talk about actions that were actively in progress (happening right then) at a specific time in the past. It shows that an action was not finished.

🚶‍♂️➡️

Action started before the time

🛑⌚🛑

Action happening *at* the specific moment

➡️🚶‍♂️

Action continued after the time

Form: Subject + was/were + Verb(-ing)

Rule A: Singular Subjects (was)

Use was for I, he, she, it, or any single person, animal, or thing.

  • 1. The deep-sea diver was examining the rare coral formation at 10:00 AM.
  • 2. While the team rested, a wild falcon was circling high above the canyon walls.

Rule B: Plural Subjects (were)

Use were for we, they, you, or multiple people, animals, or things.

  • 1. Many creative designers were drafting plans for the new theater stage all last week.
  • 2. The rescue team members were clearing the blocked pathway when the second storm arrived.

Rule C: Interrupted Action

Past Continuous is often used to show a longer background action that is interrupted by a sudden, short action (Past Simple). We often use "while" or "when".

  • 1. The students were reviewing their notes when the main speaker entered.
  • 2. She was adjusting her safety harness when the final countdown began.

Rule D: Background Setting

Past Continuous can set the scene for a story by describing what was happening in the background.

  • 1. A relentless blizzard was howling through the mountain ridge all through the night.
  • 2. Soft waves were crashing gently against the dock while the fishermen slept soundly.
💡 Grammar Tip: Watch out for compound subjects! If you have two singular subjects joined by "and" (e.g., The musician and her manager...), it is now plural. Always use were preparing, not was.

2. Collocations (Natural Word Partnerships)

Collocations are common word combinations that natively sound right to English speakers. Using them makes your writing more descriptive and professional.

✅

Natural: "Heavy rain"

≠

Unnatural: "Strong rain"

3. Unit Action Vocabulary & Key Expressions

These precise action and motion words are crucial for narrative and descriptive tasks. Learn their exact meanings and use cases.

🪂

A skydiver uses the Ripcord while Free falling.

📉

To Plummet is to drop *very* fast.

🧶🛑🧶

A safety Line keeps you from becoming Entangled.

A. Key Action Verbs for Stories

Plummet
(Verb) To fall or drop straight downward at an extremely high speed.
Context: "The temperature began to plummet as soon as the dark clouds blocked out the sun."
Entangled
(Verb/Adjective) Caught, twisted, or closely knotted together into a complex mass.
Context: "The anchor line became completely entangled in heavy seaweed beneath the boat."
Free falling
(Verb Phrase/Adjective) Moving downward strictly driven by gravity, completely unrestricted and fast.
Context: "The payload spent thirty seconds free falling before its deployment sequence kicked in."

B. Adventure Specific Terms

Ripcord
(Noun) A specific cord that is pulled to initiate the opening mechanism of a parachute.
Context: "The training jumper firmly gripped the emergency ripcord during her first simulation."
Line
(Noun) A designated length of cord, braided rope, or structural wire used for safety, control, or support.
Context: "Make sure to tie the safety line firmly around the anchor post before stepping down."

C. Narrative Idioms & Character Expressions

These idioms describe how characters feel or act in difficult situations.

Automatic motion, no effort

→

Idioms give color to actions.

😱💥

Big failure, complete loss

  • 1. Went through the motions: Performing an assigned task or obligation routinely and mechanically, entirely lacking real passion, enthusiasm, or focused interest. ("Unmotivated by the subject, the student just went through the motions during the final revision session.")
  • 2. Blown it: To completely ruin, mishandle, or throw away an excellent opportunity, chance, or advantage. ("He realized he had blown it when he accidentally deleted the only copy of his project file.")
