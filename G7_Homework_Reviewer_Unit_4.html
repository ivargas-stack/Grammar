1. Past Simple vs. Past Perfect

When discussing two connected events in the past, we use the Past Perfect for the event that occurred earlier, and the Past Simple for the event that occurred later[cite: 1].

  • Past Perfect Form: had + past participle (e.g., had left, had finished, had lived)[cite: 1]
  • Past Simple Form: Regular verb + -ed or irregular past form (e.g., arrived, began, saw)[cite: 1]

Detailed Sample Sentences:

Sample 1: "The robbers had left the bank when the policemen finally arrived."[cite: 1]
Explanation: Action 1 (robbers leaving) happened first in the past, so it uses Past Perfect (*had left*)[cite: 1]. Action 2 (police arriving) happened second, so it uses Past Simple (*arrived*)[cite: 1].
Sample 2: "Mr. Palmer hadn't spoken any Chinese before he moved to Peking."[cite: 1]
Explanation: His lack of Chinese speech (Action 1) occurred before his relocation to Peking (Action 2)[cite: 1].
Sample 3: "When the rain started, the Atkinsons had finished planting trees."[cite: 1]
Explanation: The planting was completely completed (Action 1: *had finished*) before the rain began (Action 2: *started*)[cite: 1].
Sample 4: "Darcy had seen this castle before she came again last Sunday."[cite: 1]
Explanation: Her first visit (Action 1) happened prior to her visit last Sunday (Action 2)[cite: 1].

2. Verb-Noun Collocations

A collocation refers to a group of words that naturally co-occur together in standard English[cite: 1]. Verbs must pair correctly with specific nouns[cite: 1].

Detailed Sample Sentences:

Sample 1: "The contestants had to perform several tasks like hitting a target and jumping hurdles."[cite: 1]
Explanation: The verb perform naturally collocates with the noun tasks when executing specific assigned activities[cite: 1].
Sample 2: "Our team celebrated their victory by swimming in the fountain."[cite: 1]
Explanation: We celebrate a victory (win), rather than "making a victory" or "doing a victory"[cite: 1].
Sample 3: "Athletes need to pay attention when officials give instructions."[cite: 1]
Explanation: The verb pay pairs with attention to mean listening or watching carefully[cite: 1].
Sample 4: "Siti was awarded a medal for her bravery."[cite: 1]
Explanation: A medal is the specific noun used for formal honors given for bravery or athletic achievements[cite: 1].

3. Action Verbs & Definitions

Action verbs communicate physical or structural activities clearly in real-life diaries and narratives[cite: 1].

Detailed Sample Sentences:

Sample 1: "Many students chose to take part in the annual sports tournament."[cite: 1]
Meaning: To be involved in or participate in an activity[cite: 1].
Sample 2: "The organization decided to give away free books to young readers."[cite: 1]
Meaning: To freely offer or distribute something without asking for payment[cite: 1].
Sample 3: "The drama club worked hard to perform a theatrical play on the last day of the week."[cite: 1]
Meaning: To carry out an action, pattern of behavior, or stage presentation[cite: 1].
Sample 4: "Families gather together every autumn to celebrate the harvest festival."[cite: 1]
Meaning: To mark an occasion or anniversary with festivities[cite: 1].
