Unit 8: Summing You Up

Grammar Focus: Conjunctions of Addition and Purpose

The Goal of a Summary

When writing a summary, your primary objective is to be concise. You must pack maximum information into minimal words without losing the original meaning.

Instead of writing choppy, repetitive sentences, expert writers use specific tools:

  • Conjunctions of Addition: To link related facts together and remove repeated subjects or verbs.
  • Conjunctions of Purpose: To clearly explain why an action occurred in a single, flowing clause.

What Exactly is a Conjunction?

A conjunction is a connecting word. In English grammar, conjunctions are specialized words used to link other words, phrases, or full independent clauses together to form complex, flowing sentences.

In Grade 7, we categorize these connectors by what they achieve in your writing:

  • Addition: Shows the reader that you are adding more evidence or details that are equal in importance to what you just said.
  • Purpose: Answers the hidden "Why?" in the reader's mind by linking an action to its intended goal.
Without Conjunctions (Choppy) With Conjunctions (Flowing)
The school raised money. They bought new computers. They wanted students to learn coding. The school raised money and bought new computers so that students could learn coding.

Conjunctions of addition link related ideas. They signal to the reader: "Here is more information on the same topic."

as well as Connects two actions or facts, usually keeping the focus on the main subject.

Verb Form Rule: Parallelism & Addition

Choosing the right verb form is critical when adding information:

  • The Balance Rule (Parallelism): For paired conjunctions (not only... but also, both... and), the verbs on both sides must match in tense and form.
    ✔️ She not only studied math but also practiced piano.
    ❌ She not only studied math but also was a piano player.
  • The "-ing" Rule for As well as: When connecting verbs with "as well as", the verb immediately following it must be in the -ing form (gerund).
    ✔️ As well as practicing daily, the team ate healthy food.

Part 2: Conjunctions of Purpose (Using Modals)

Purpose conjunctions explain the goal or reason behind an action.

Conjunction Grammar Requirement Your Turn: Type a sentence, then check the model!
so that Must be followed by a subject + a modal verb (can, could, will, would, may, might).
Rangers tagged the turtles so that scientists could track them.
in order that A highly formal version of so that. Also requires a subject + a modal verb.
The school extended breaks in order that students might join clubs.

Verb Form Rule: Modals vs. Base Verbs for Purpose

Never mix up your purpose verb forms! Remember this quick checklist:

  • Use a Modal Verb (can, could, will, would) after so that and in order that.
    (e.g., ...so that they could win.)
  • Use a Base Verb (infinitive) after in order to and so as to.
    (e.g., ...in order to win.)
