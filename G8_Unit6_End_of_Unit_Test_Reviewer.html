Understanding Pronouns

A pronoun is a word that replaces a noun so we do not have to repeat the same word again and again.

Type 1: Relative Pronouns

These pronouns connect a noun to a clause that gives more information about it.

Pronoun Used For Easy Example
who People (Subject) The boy who smiled is my brother.
whom People (Object) The girl whom I met yesterday was helpful.
whose Possession / Ownership The teacher whose car was parked outside is here.
which Things / Animals The dog which barked all night is quiet now.
where Places This is the house where I was born.
that People, Things, Animals The book that you gave me was full of fun stories.

Type 2: Demonstrative Pronouns

These pronouns point directly to specific things near or far.

  • This (Near, 1 item): This is my pencil in my hand.
  • That (Far, 1 item): That is a tall tree over there.
  • These (Near, 2+ items): These are my books on my desk.
  • Those (Far, 2+ items): Those are birds in the sky.

Type 3: Indefinite Pronouns

These pronouns refer to people, things, or places in a general way, without saying exactly who or what they are.

  • People: someone, anybody, nobody, everybody
  • Things: something, anything, nothing, everything
Easy Rule: Words starting with no- (nobody, nothing) mean 0. Words starting with every- mean all!
💡 Teacher's Tip for ESL Learners: Look at the word right before the blank! If it is a person (e.g., scientist, boy), use who or whose. If it is a place (e.g., village, house), use where!

Punctuation: Mastering Apostrophes (')

Apostrophes have two main jobs in Grade 8 English:

Job 1: Contractions (Missing Letters)

We use an apostrophe when we join two words together and remove one or more letters.

  • do + not = don't (the apostrophe replaces 'o')
  • she + is = she's (the apostrophe replaces 'i')
  • cannot = can't (replaces 'no')
  • should + not = shouldn't

Job 2: Possession (Showing Ownership)

We use an apostrophe to show that something belongs to a person, animal, or group.

Rule A: Singular Owner (1 Person/Thing) → Add 's

• The tail of the dog → The dog's tail.
• The brother of Jack → Jack's brother.

Rule B: Regular Plural Owners (2+ People/Things ending in 's') → Add only '

• The room for many teachers → The teachers' lounge.
• The house of my grandparents → My grandparents' house.

Rule C: Irregular Plural Owners (2+ People NOT ending in 's') → Add 's

• The toys belonging to children → The children's toys.
• The bags belonging to women → The women's bags.
